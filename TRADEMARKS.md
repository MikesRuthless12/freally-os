# Trademarks and the Freally brand

Freally OS's **code** is open source under the Apache License 2.0. The **brand** is not.

## What stays Mike's

- The name **Freally**, the names **Freally OS**, **Freally Core**, **Freally Stage**, **Freally UI** and every other Freally product name.
- The Freally logo and wordmark.
- The Freally hardware set (every Blender-rendered key, knob, fader, switch, meter and well), the Freally UI kit, and Freally OS's own renders, wallpapers, icons and fonts' designs. These are covered by [`assets/LICENSE-BRAND.md`](assets/LICENSE-BRAND.md).

Apache-2.0 § 6 already says the licence grants no right to use these names and marks. This file says it plainly.

## If you fork Freally OS

You're welcome to: that's what the licence is for. A fork must:

1. **Use another name.** Don't call it Freally, Freally OS or anything that could be mistaken for it.
2. **Remove the brand assets** (everything under `assets/` that `assets/LICENSE-BRAND.md` covers) and use your own.
3. **Sign with your own key.** Official Freally OS machines trust only Mike's release key, and they'll refuse anything signed by another one. That's the seal working as designed.

## Official builds

Only builds signed with Mike's release key are Freally OS. Builds are reproducible, so anyone can rebuild an official image from this source and check its SHA-256.
